## OrcaCyber's abliterated Hy4-flash GGUF — unchanged since 11-21; still waiting on one gate click

Source: [post](https://x.com/OrcaCyberHQ/status/2124053590096630211) (2026-11-21, quoting [this](https://x.com/OrcaCyberHQ/status/2123100565559562434)), [press release](https://prtimes.jp/main/html/rd/p/000000144.000138449.html). Weights: [orcarouter/Hy4-flash-abliterated-GGUF](https://huggingface.co/orcarouter/Hy4-flash-abliterated-GGUF) at `3fd6454b8b1d179d532f8b1635ac7b284733e20c`, base Tencent's Hy4-flash, licence the Tencent Hy License (the repo's `license_name` is `tencent-hy-license`; the licence text carries no territory clause). The earlier follow-up with the full fit arithmetic and the Kullback–Leibler numbers is in [2026-11-21](2026-11-21-followup.md).

**What it is.** Refusal-reduced (abliterated) conversions of Hy4-flash to GGUFs in stock llama.cpp types. Hy4-flash is a 224-billion-parameter mixture-of-experts with about 63–64 billion of those parameters in an Engram lookup table and about 11–12 billion active per token, a million-token context, and a hash table that is read on demand rather than held resident. Two builds are published: Mix-128G in three files and Mix-256G in six.

**What the repo says today, re-read from the API.** The revision is still `3fd6454b`, last modified 2026-11-21 09:14 UTC — nothing has been re-uploaded in the three days since we first looked. Mix-128G is three files of 47,842,789,088 + 47,234,568,768 + 42,872,611,744 bytes, which is 137,949,969,600 bytes (128.5 GiB), plus an `imatrix.gguf` of 809,740,800 bytes. Mix-256G is six files totalling 238,217,368,224 bytes (221.9 GiB). The repo's whole catalogue is 612,044,148,992 bytes. The repository is still gated with automatic approval, and a range request for the first shard with our account's token still returns 403 — the gate has not been accepted.

**Engine support.** Hy4-flash is still not in mainline llama.cpp's supported architectures; support is an open draft, [PR #32517 "model : add hy_v4_flash support"](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/pull/32517). Our image was built at its head `cf70454` on 11-21 and the pack was made against that build. The PR moved today — its head is now `a09e62422fe914cdc6b724a778efa226f34179f9` and it is still open. A newer head can rename tensors, so when this download does happen the first load decides between the image we have and a rebuild at the current head; it is not safe to assume the old image reads the files.

**Verdict: unchanged — watch, and it is the download that already has a place to land.** The maths from 11-21 still holds: Mix-128G at about 4.84 bits per weight is roughly 94 GB resident with the Engram table paged, which fits the experts-in-RAM tier of about 160 GB across 56 GB of video memory and 125 GB of main memory; Mix-256G does not. Its value is a Hy4-flash that is both less trigger-happy on refusals and a couple of bits denser than the Q3_K we already serve on one card, so a comparison against that build is the interesting test.

What changed is storage, not the model. The archive is at 344 GB free, under its 500 GB headroom rule, so this does not go there; the overflow disk now stands at 983 GB free and takes the 138 GB pack. The build SSD, which is where engine images are made, has about 20 GB free of a terabyte, so an engine rebuild is not something to start today either.

What has not changed is the one step only Alex can take: accepting the gate on the repository page, which is a single click on an automatic-approval gate and costs nothing. Once that is done, the shards pull to the overflow disk, the hashes are checked against the Hub's own values, and the file gets a viability case: load and fit at 100K, an oracle answer, decode and first-token rates, retrieval near 100K, the short quality stage, and a comparison against the Q3_K Hy4-flash build already qualified here. Until then this stays where it is: researched, staged, not executed.

## Unsloth can now fine-tune Inkling 2 Mini — a training path for a model we already serve that fits one 24 GB card

Source: [post](https://x.com/UnslothAI/status/2125243469094605071) (2026-11-24 15:01 UTC), [fine-tuning guide](https://unsloth.ai/docs/models/inkling-2-mini/fine-tuning) (the release links `finetuning-inkling-2-mini`; both addresses serve the same page), and the [v0.1.924-beta release](https://github.com/unslothai/unsloth/releases/tag/v0.1.924-beta), published 2026-11-24 14:38 UTC as "Inkling finetuning + perf fixes".

**What it is.** LoRA and QLoRA training for Thinking Machines Lab's Inkling 2 Mini — the 26.4B mixture-of-experts with a looped transformer we qualified for serving on 2026-11-21 (127 tok/s short decode, 105.8 tok/s through a 96K prompt, a correct image row and retrieval near 100K). The new part is that a fine-tune now fits a single 24 GB card, includes the vision layers, supports tensor, pipeline and context parallel across cards, and exports either a merged 16-bit model or a GGUF that stock llama.cpp runs.

**What we checked.** The checkpoint Unsloth trains from is [unsloth/Inkling-2-Mini](https://huggingface.co/unsloth/Inkling-2-Mini) at `ae70da4f4059a637c28f393ffce5c5e81e70c7c0`, Apache-2.0, 20.2 GB in 14 files: a single 19,526,019,168-byte safetensors of 8-bit model-opt weights, a 635,801,416-byte MTP head, and tokenizer files — byte-for-byte the same sizes as the upstream [thinkingmachines/Inkling-2-Mini](https://huggingface.co/thinkingmachines/Inkling-2-Mini) at `c9d028302cd0f035fdf8e7e91f089a4ce94d2195`, also Apache-2.0 and ungated. Unsloth re-quantises the 128 routed experts per layer to 4-bit while loading, leaving about 14.5 GB resident for the weights, and keeps the looped layers' message gates in 16-bit; the guide is explicit that plain 4-bit transformers training quantises those gates and produces wrong initial losses (3.4–3.9 instead of 0.4–1.2).

The memory claim holds up in their own numbers: 21.1 GB peak on a 24 GB card for QLoRA at batch 1 with four gradient-accumulation steps, 29.4 GiB against 58.3 GiB for transformers plus TRL on an A100 at 2,048 tokens, and a two-T4 Kaggle notebook at about 26–27 seconds per step. That is a fit on our 24 GB card by roughly 3 GB — but only with nothing else on it, and today that card is serving a resident model at 23.2 GB while the daily-driver suites run.

It is not a two-card job here. FlexAttention is used on Ampere and newer; the free-tier recipe runs T4 (Turing) with eager attention, and nothing in the guide promises Volta. Our 32 GB card is sm_70, so the training run is one-card only, and it holds that card exclusively for its duration — the opposite of the side-by-side pattern the serving work uses.

Export is the other constraint. A merged 16-bit model is about 53 GB on disk, and the GGUF path writes that 53 GB merge and a 53 GB 16-bit GGUF before the 17.48 GB `q4_k_m` (their Colab A100 run took about 21 minutes for the export), so the guide budgets about 150 GB of free disk. Our build SSD is down to roughly 20 GB of its terabyte and the model archive sits at 344 GB free, under the 500 GB headroom rule; the overflow disk has 983 GB free, which is the realistic landing place for an export.

One release note does not apply to us: "fixed incorrect max context length (2-3x faster inference)" is about Unsloth's own inference stack. We serve Inkling 2 Mini as a GGUF through llama.cpp, so our measured context behaviour does not change.

**Verdict: try, researched and backlogged — nothing downloaded, trained or queued, and no card window booked.** The value is that this is the first family where the lab could go from measuring a model to tuning it at home and exporting back into the engine we already run: tool-call format, house coding style, or a refusal profile closer to what the household actually asks for, then a `q4_k_m` GGUF served beside the existing Inkling entry and compared against it on our own cases. The costs are concrete: the model card's own numbers put the run at roughly 21 GB of the 24 GB card with the card exclusive, the export needs a 150 GB staging area, and a training recipe is a new class of workload here — the one earlier fine-tune plan in the lab (a speculative-decoding drafter) was written and never run.

The blocker is scheduling, not research. The 24 GB card belongs to the daily-driver testing work right now and carries a resident model; the general model queue is paused by an operator marker on the serving host. So the next step is a decision the serving and scheduling side owns: when the current suite ends, take one card window, pull the 20.2 GB checkpoint to a disk the GPU host can read, train a short adapter on a small public set, export, and measure the result against the existing Inkling 2 Mini entry on the same case.
