## Edge0 145B and 120B: backlog, high interest

- **What it is:** two latent mixture-of-experts models from [Edge0-AI/Edge0](https://github.com/Edge0-AI/Edge0)
  (release [v0.2.0](https://github.com/Edge0-AI/Edge0/releases/tag/v0.2.0), Nov 11), built for one consumer
  NVIDIA GPU. The part Iris's note leaves out, and the reason it matters to us: Hugging Face lists both as
  **derived from Qwen3.8-Flash-Next**, which is our daily driver. Weights:
  [Edge0-145B-q3k](https://huggingface.co/Edge0/Edge0-145B-q3k) (revision `2d801163`, 105.4 GB),
  [Edge0-145B-NVFP4](https://huggingface.co/Edge0/Edge0-145B-NVFP4) (`cf9b3679`, 112.9 GB) and
  [Edge0-120B-q3k](https://huggingface.co/Edge0/Edge0-120B-q3k) (`d8a85821`, 92.3 GB). None are gated.
- **How it works:** it keeps Flash-Next's skeleton: 48 layers (36 linear attention, plus indexed attention every
  fourth layer), a 51.2-billion-parameter n-gram embedding table (PLE) in fp8 that is memory-mapped, and one
  multi-token-prediction layer for speculative decoding. The change is in the experts. Each layer has 512 experts,
  10 routed plus one shared per token. A shared down-projection squeezes the token from 2560 to 1792 dimensions
  (1280 on the 120B) before the experts run, and an up-projection restores it afterwards. That makes every expert a
  small fixed-size record, about 1.6 MB, so the engine keeps a few thousand on the card and streams the rest from
  host memory over PCIe. The 145B's expert weights total 40.8 GiB in Q3_K. Upstream measured them as 23.1 GiB VRAM
  plus 31.3 GiB RAM on a 24 GB card, with the PLE table mapped on top.
- **Engine:** `edge0-strata`, a fork of [Strata](https://github.com/Niko1221/Strata) (MIT; the fork's merge base
  is upstream's Oct 31 commit) that adds latent-expert kernels and CPU/PCIe scheduling. `edge0-infer serve`
  launches it. On Linux it is a source build only: CUDA 13, ggml from a pinned llama.cpp commit, and compute
  capability 7.5 or newer. On the GPU host that means **the RTX 3090 only. The V100 is sm_70 and cannot run it.** the GPU host's
  driver already reports CUDA 13.0, so a CUDA 13 build container is enough. No host change is needed.
- **Licence:** the engine is Apache-2.0 / MIT. The weights use the Edge0 Community Model License 1.0: free to use,
  run and study, with a commercial reservation only for selling the model, paid inference services and paid
  coding/office assistant products. Qwen's licence still applies underneath. Our lab use is fine.
- **Upstream claims (not measured here):** 145B Q3_K at 121.2 tok/s agent decode and 3,555 tok/s 16K prefill on an
  RTX 5090 D; 91.7 tok/s on an RTX 4090. Card benchmarks: terminal-bench 2.1 86.5 and deepswe 64.6, both above the
  card's own Flash-Next row.
- **Limits we found in its docs:** concurrent requests wait in one queue, with no parallel sessions. Decoding is not
  bit-repeatable at temperature 0. So as shipped it cannot be our multi-session agent backend. It is a
  single-session speed and quality challenger to our Flash-Next UD-Q2_K_XL setup.
- **Verdict:** worth testing, deferred to the backlog because of the port priority. When the pause lifts, the plan
  is: build the engine for sm_86 on the agent host, pull the 145B Q3_K to the NAS (it fits: the GPU host has 125 GB of RAM), then
  run a viability case on the 3090 with the same checks as our Flash-Next baseline. That covers tokens/s, time to
  first token, retrieval near 100K and our quality stage, followed by the coding set and an agent run through
  T3 Code/OpenCode. The 120B comes second.

## MiniMax-H3-Image: excluded, licence

- **What it is:** [MiniMaxAI/MiniMax-H3-Image](https://huggingface.co/MiniMaxAI/MiniMax-H3-Image) (revision
  `5f904730`), a 33.1B single-stream DiT for text-to-image and editing, distilled to 6 steps. Its text encoder is
  MiniMax-H3's Qwen3-VL encoder (66.7 GB in BF16). The community quants make it small enough for our hardware:
  [unsloth GGUF](https://huggingface.co/unsloth/MiniMax-H3-Image-GGUF) runs from Q4_K_M 12.7 GB to Q8_0 22.0 GB,
  and [Comfy-Org's repack](https://huggingface.co/Comfy-Org/MiniMax-H3-Image) has int8 (27.1 GB) and NVFP4
  (15.7 GB) encoders.
- **Why not:** the MiniMax-H3-Image Community License limits all use to an "Applicable Territory" of worldwide
  *excluding the EU, the UK, the Republic of Korea and the United States*. We are in the US, so the licence grants
  us nothing. The quants carry the same licence. Recorded as an exclusion; we revisit only if the licence changes.

## FlashTopK: no value on our GPUs

- **What it is:** [Tencent-Hunyuan/FlashTopK](https://github.com/Tencent-Hunyuan/FlashTopK) (Apache-2.0), from the
  HyScale paper ([arXiv 2610.32057](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.32057)). It is an exact, deterministic fused top-k
  kernel for learned sparse-attention indexers.
- **How it works:** a DSA-style indexer scores every earlier key for each query, and the model attends only to the
  top k. FlashTopK skips materialising the score table: it estimates each row's threshold from a strided sample,
  prunes keys whose score bound cannot reach it, then rescores the rows the sample missed, so the answer stays the
  true top-k. It also replays the selection during recomputation in training. The paper reports 1.7–3.3x over dense
  scoring plus FlashInfer top-k on trained indexers.
- **Why not for us:** it supports Hopper (SM90) and SM100/SM103 only, in Python with Triton and CuTe DSL, for
  token-level indexers (GLM-5.x, Hy4-preview). Block-level selection for DeepSeek-V4's CSA and Flash-Next's QSA is
  only on its roadmap. None of it runs on an RTX 3090 (sm_86) or a V100 (sm_70). We watch the roadmap.

## Project Maya v1.1.62: no change for us

- **What it is:** [mw00/project-maya](https://github.com/mw00/project-maya) (MIT),
  [v1.1.62](https://github.com/mw00/project-maya/releases/tag/v1.1.62) (Nov 13). Its headline feature lets an AMD
  Strix/Gorgon Halo APU and an NVIDIA GPU share one model on Linux. The APU's memory holds the single copy of the
  weights, and only activations cross between the two GPUs. Upstream's test box was an APU plus a V100 32 GB in a
  USB4 dock, which gave GLM-5.3-Flash 32K prefill up to 3x and DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash decode +18%.
  [v1.1.63](https://github.com/mw00/project-maya/releases/tag/v1.1.63) (Nov 14) fixes the Windows DeepSeek build and
  a GLM restart loop on that setup.
- **Verdict:** the GPU host has no AMD APU, so neither release changes anything we run. We stay on our pinned Maya build,
  and engine upgrades are paused anyway.

## Tess-4.5-594B-A17B: does not fit the GPU host

- **What it is:** [migtissera/Tess-4.5-594B-A17B](https://huggingface.co/migtissera/Tess-4.5-594B-A17B) (Apache-2.0,
  Nov 13), the Tess agentic-coding finetune of Thinking Machines'
  [Inkling 2 Small](https://huggingface.co/thinkingmachines/Inkling-2-Small): a 594B mixture-of-experts model with 17B
  active parameters, a 1M-token window and image input. The finetune is a LoRA merged into the attention projections
  and output head; the experts are the base model's, unchanged.
- **How the claim reads:** 88.2% on SWE-Bench Pro v2 Hard against 86.3% for the base, on 51 tasks. That is roughly one
  extra task solved, with about 9% fewer output tokens. Long-context scores are level. We did not measure any of it.
- **What we checked:** the repo is 394 GB in NVFP4/FP8. Nobody has made a GGUF or EXL3 of Tess yet. The base model's
  smallest Unsloth GGUF (1-bit UD-IQ1_S) is 171.7 GB, and a Tess one would be the same size. the GPU host has 56 GB of GPU
  memory plus 125 GB of RAM, and our experts-in-RAM tier tops out around 160 GB of weights once the OS, a 100K-token
  KV cache and buffers are counted. llama.cpp also lacks the `inkling2` architecture so far (open
  [PR #32187](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/pull/32187)).
- **Verdict:** no local fit, even at 1 bit. The base model is on OpenRouter's free tier and is already on our hosted
  test list, which is where an Inkling-family number will come from. Tess itself is not on OpenRouter.
- **Backlog:** the smaller sibling [Tess-4.5-27B](https://huggingface.co/migtissera/Tess-4.5-27B) (a Qwen3.8-27B
  finetune, Apache-2.0) fits the RTX 3090 easily (Q4_K_M 16.6 GB, with an MTP draft head). It goes in the backlog as a
  finetune comparison against our Qwen3.8-27B baseline once testing resumes. Not downloaded.
