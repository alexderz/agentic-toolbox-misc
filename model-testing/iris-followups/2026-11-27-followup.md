## Raon-1 mini, out of preview — try, staged

Source: [post](https://x.com/Krafton_AI/status/2125292128884654433), [weights](https://huggingface.co/KRAFTON/Raon-1-mini), [GGUF](https://huggingface.co/KRAFTON/Raon-1-mini-gguf).

**What it is.** KRAFTON's 28.8B-parameter mixture-of-experts trained from scratch, 4.3B active (6 of 128 routed experts plus one shared expert), Apache-2.0. A 25-layer hybrid: 18 Raon Delta Attention layers with a fixed-size state and 7 gated-MLA layers that keep a KV cache, about 8 KiB per token in BF16, which is why 262,144 native context is cheap here. Six reasoning-effort levels, of which five are applied with steering vectors shipped in the checkpoint.

**What we checked.** The GGUF repository is public and fetchable without a login (a range request on the Q4_K_M returned 206), at revision `5369b6d7a035`, Apache-2.0, nine files. Q4_K_M is 18.33 GiB, IQ4_XS 15.1 GiB, Q5_K_M 20.66 GiB, Q8_0 28.59 GiB, plus a 0.68 GiB DFlash2 drafter. A header read of Q4_K_M gives architecture `raon1`, 25 blocks, 128 experts with 6 used, context 262,144, 259,840-token vocabulary, 736 tensors. On the 24 GB card that is roughly 20 GB resident at 100K with an 8-bit KV cache, comfortably inside the 512 MiB-per-card floor.

**The catch is the engine.** Upstream llama.cpp merged Raon-1 mini support on 2026-11-25 ([PR #32176](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/pull/32176)); the first release carrying it is b12732, and master is at b12823 today. Our newest mainline image is b12633, which is 125 commits behind that merge, so a fresh build is the prerequisite. Two things are not upstream and worth saying plainly: the reasoning-effort steering vectors and the DFlash2 drafter both live in [KRAFTON's own llama.cpp fork](https://github.com/krafton-ai/llama.cpp), and the upstream model file has no steering or drafter code in it. A stock build runs the base model with its chat template; the six effort levels and the drafter are not something a stock server honours.

**Verdict: try — the strongest new model in this note.** Apache-2.0, one-card fit at useful context, and it is the first model here built on Korean-lab data with agentic tool use on the label. Next steps, in order: a mainline llama.cpp build at or after b12732 for both card generations, the 19.3 GB pull, then a text viability case (fit and load, oracle, decode and first-token rates, retrieval near 100K, the short quality stage). Case `0mzb` is staged in the lab's candidate file with that exact shape.

## Inkling-2-Mini-Unbound v3 — try, staged

Source: [post](https://x.com/TeksEdge/status/2125424242045194364), [weights](https://huggingface.co/nokeepai/Inkling-2-Mini-Unbound), [GGUF](https://huggingface.co/nokeepai/Inkling-2-Mini-Unbound-GGUF), [code](https://github.com/nokeepai/inkling-2-mini-unbound).

**What it is.** A refusal-reduced fine-tune of Thinking Machines' Inkling 2 Mini, the 26B MoE we qualified for serving on 2026-11-21. Apache-2.0, not gated. The post's 0.8% to 85.2% refusal figures describe v2.1; the current version is v3, released 2026-11-27, which adds the "Prime" adapter step that makes the model stop deliberating and act. That is the part that matters to us: the original Mini's habit of thinking through a whole turn and writing no code is exactly the failure our agent tests punish.

**What we checked.** The repository is at `accd51aa`, Apache-2.0, 507 GB of files across five versions. v3 Q4_K_M is 17,782,517,280 bytes (16.56 GiB) and the image projector is 345,910,944 bytes (0.32 GiB). The card asks for a llama.cpp build with the Inkling 2 loop fix from PR #32704, commit `19bc41dbb`, and says tagged releases up to b12608 do not have it; our b12633 image is 11 commits past that commit, so it should load — a claim only a load will settle.

**Verdict: try, and the comparison is clean.** Same engine, same quant level, different weights: the stock Mini's UD-Q4_K_XL pack on the same b12633 build is the control, then Unbound v3 Q4_K_M. The runner has no image stage yet, so the first row is text-only and the projector is downloaded but unused until that exists. What the row can show is speed, fit, retrieval and the short quality stage; the refusal and over-thinking claims need a graded agent run to mean anything. Case `0mza` is staged.

Note the settings the publisher pins: temperature 0.6, top-p 1.0, reasoning effort 0.9, thinking capped at 4,096 tokens. Zero temperature made earlier versions loop inside their thinking.

## Tencent Hy4 flash local quants (AngelSlim, llama.cpp, Strata) — watch

Source: [post](https://x.com/TencentHunyuan/status/2125510109417857368), [GGUF](https://huggingface.co/AngelSlim/Hy4-flash-GGUF), [Strata instructions](https://huggingface.co/AngelSlim/Hy4-flash-GGUF/blob/main/README_strata.md).

**What it is.** Tencent's own compression toolkit publishing Hy4-flash GGUFs with Strata launch instructions and two patches, plus a `hy4-flash-hpc` branch in Strata for larger machines (head `6a6db7ff`, 2026-11-23; Strata main has since moved to `4560d03`). The licence file is the Tencent Hy License, the same text we checked before for the abliterated pack: Apache-2.0 plus added commercial terms for large model-as-a-service providers, no territory clause.

**The arithmetic.** The repository is 1.09 TB. The packs that suit this box: Q4_K_M with built-in MTP at 153.5 GiB, the same with 25% of experts pruned at 131.7 GiB, Q3_K at 144.7 GiB, IQ2_AH at 125.7 GiB. Their own guide wants a GPU with 16 GiB and at least 128 GiB of host RAM, because host RAM holds a complete copy of the trunk experts (83.4 GiB for the full model) unless you split them across VRAM and RAM.

**Verdict: watch.** We already serve Hy4-flash on the 24 GB card (Q3_K, ~43 tok/s short decode, ~1,400 tok/s prefill at depth). A Q4_K_M with MTP could plausibly beat it on quality and drafting, but 153.5 GiB does not fit the host's 125 GB of RAM at the documented configuration, the pruned pack is the honest candidate at 131.7 GiB, and either one is a 130-155 GB download against storage we do not have free today. Worth revisiting when the archive expansion lands and if one of the Hy4 packs we already hold starts losing to it.

## Nemotron-Labs-Image-30B-A3B — no fit, no engine

Source: [post](https://x.com/ItsmeAjayKV/status/2125574724613034131), [weights](https://huggingface.co/nvidia/Nemotron-Labs-Image-30B-A3B), [paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31358).

NVIDIA's open-weight autoregressive text-to-image MoE, 30B total and 3B active, initialised from Nemotron 3 Nano, with a bundled 642M-parameter image tokenizer. The repository is 133.19 GB across 32 shards plus the tokenizer checkpoint, public, and licensed under the NVIDIA License for non-commercial research and evaluation only.

**Verdict: no.** The card's worked example runs on four 80 GB H100s with vLLM. It is 133 GB against our 56 GB of video memory and 125 GB of RAM, it is an autoregressive image model rather than the diffusion models our image path serves, and the runner has no image-generation stage at all. Add a non-commercial licence and there is nothing here for us today.

## Plato's Key (Stanford Hazy Research) — watch, no code

Source: [post](https://x.com/Alissonerdx/status/2126024817573011490), [write-up](https://hazyresearch.stanford.edu/blog/2026-11-25-platos-key).

The page is live and reads as Iris describes: a closed-form orthogonal-Procrustes rotation that aligns hidden-state "keys" between two frozen models, fitted on fewer than 5,000 facts both models recall, with no training. Reported medians over 20 model pairs: 41% transfer accuracy against a 55% self-swap reference, chained maps A→B→C keeping 99% of a direct map, and a 256-component map keeping 74% of the full one.

**Verdict: watch.** There is no paper and no repository linked, only the blog post, so there is nothing to run. If code appears, the interesting question here is narrow: whether a rotation fitted on a small model transfers recall to a bigger one on the same family, which is a cheap experiment on our two cards.

## OpenGame — no value for the model lab

Source: [post](https://x.com/tuturetom/status/2125885818816561502), [repo](https://github.com/nexu-io/opengame).

An Apache-2.0 TypeScript "super game agent" that drives 22 local coding agents into a game-studio pipeline (code, art, 3D, audio, playtest, publish) with 25 playable templates and export paths for WeChat, TikTok, Douyin, Telegram and itch.io. 2,455 stars in two days; the CI pins Node 26, and it wants Chrome for screenshots and FFmpeg for trailers. No releases yet.

**Verdict: no value here.** It is a harness around agents rather than a model, it needs a desktop toolchain we do not keep on the GPU host, and its output is small games, not measurements. If anyone wants to try it, it belongs on a workstation, not in the lab.

## Cortheon v2 — try later, not installed

Source: [post](https://x.com/ashxhart/status/2126076517356609642), [repo](https://github.com/ashhart/Cortheon).

MIT, Python, 111 stars, v2.4.0 published 2026-11-27. A runtime that wraps a frozen local model in execution-verified parallel consensus: candidate answers are checked by running Python, SymPy or Z3 scripts, optionally refuted in a second wave, with no weight updates and no cloud API. It sits in front of llama.cpp, Ollama, vLLM, SGLang, MLX or any OpenAI-compatible server and sandboxes candidate code with `bwrap`.

**Verdict: try later.** It is the rare "cool tech" item that could change a number we already measure: the README reports +16.7 to +30.0 points on AIME 2025 at six or seven calls per problem, on the model's own outputs, and our test 7 and short quality stages are the natural place to check whether a small local model gets better or just slower. Nothing installed; it needs a model on a card and a gap.

## Magnitude — watch, now real, and its own numbers are behind ours

Source: [post](https://x.com/tomgreenwald/status/2125348896805175791), [repo](https://github.com/magnitudedev/magnitude), [docs](https://docs.magnitude.dev/models).

Since we last looked on 2026-11-23 (announced, not shipped), Magnitude has shipped: Apache-2.0, Rust, 9,109 stars, CLI 0.3.7 on 2026-11-27, and the repo now describes the SSD tiering it promised. It places shared layers and hot experts in VRAM, frequent experts in RAM, the rest on NVMe with prefetch, tunes kernels per chip, and pairs models with a drafter. Linux, macOS, Windows, Docker, OpenAI-compatible API, and one-click wiring into Hermes among other agents.

**What it runs is the limit.** Its supported families are Qwen 3.5–3.8, Gemma 4, LFM 2.5/3, Nemotron 3/3.5, Muse Glimmer, DeepSeek V4.1 and GLM-5.3-Flash. Inkling 2 and Hy4-flash are not on the list, and those two carry most of our 24 GB card's work. On the families it does run, its own hardware page puts Qwen 3.8 Flash Next 2-bit at 54 tok/s on an RTX 3090 with 64 GB of RAM and a PCIe 4.0 SSD; our Strata entry measured 120.7 tok/s on the same card for the same family, so its headline is less than half of what we already get.

**Verdict: watch.** The one place it could matter is DeepSeek V4.1 Flash at a size we cannot hold in RAM today, and that needs a fast NVMe bank we do not have free. Nothing installed, no reason to move off Strata or Maya.
