## webAI TwIL-8B (reasoning model on Granite 4.2-8B): test

- **What it is:** [webAI-Official/TwIL-8B](https://huggingface.co/webAI-Official/TwIL-8B). It is an 8.8B dense
  `granite` model (40 layers, 8 KV heads, 131,072 context) post-trained by webAI from IBM's Granite 4.2-8B with SFT
  and then GRPO/DAPO reinforcement learning. It comes in a "balanced" variant (the default) and a "verbose" one.
  The licence is the webAI Non-Commercial License 1.0, which allows our personal testing.
- **What we checked:** revision `9563ba7d`. The official GGUFs are a Q4_K_M per variant (5.35 GB) and, for the
  verbose variant, Q8_0 (9.35 GB) and F16 (17.6 GB). At 128K context the q8_0 K/V cache is about 10 GiB. That
  puts the largest official file that fits the 3090 with room to spare at Q8_0. F16 would fit only on the V100.
  llama.cpp has supported the architecture for a long time.
- **Started:** downloaded the balanced Q4_K_M and the verbose Q8_0 (both SHA-256 verified). Two viability cases
  are queued on the 3090 at 128K: `0jc` (balanced Q4_K_M) and `0jd` (verbose Q8_0). Each runs a fit check, an
  oracle, speed benchmarks on prose, code and JSON, a three-task quality check, and needle retrieval at 30K and
  120K tokens. The label claims coding, so `0jc` also runs our two one-shot coding builds (an orrery and a pagoda
  scene).
- **Results:** pending. Numbers will be added here.

## BeeLlama.cpp preview-v0.5.0 with SINQ quantization: watch

- **What it is:** [Anbeeld/beellama.cpp](https://github.com/Anbeeld/beellama.cpp) (MIT), a llama.cpp fork
  focused on KV-cache precision (KVarN) and low-bit quants. Preview v0.5.0 adds SINQ (Sinkhorn-normalized
  quantization). SINQ rescales the input columns of a tensor before the usual GGUF codecs quantize it, and keeps
  the scales in companion tensors. Only BeeLlama reads those companions. The release notes are blunt that other
  engines load the weights but give wrong output.
- **Local lens:** SINQ quantization currently targets Qwen3.5-architecture models, and Qwen3.8-27B is that
  architecture. The published demo is Bartowski's Qwen 3.5 9B recipes. The author says larger models such as
  Qwen 3.8 27B come next. The preview is a rolling build whose assets get replaced, so it is a moving target.
- **Verdict: watch.** We are waiting for SINQ GGUFs of Qwen3.8-27B or a stable v0.5.x. Then the test is a direct
  A/B against the same-size stock quant on our llama.cpp build: speed, quality and KL divergence. Added to our
  engine watchlist.

## Ash Hart's "~700 tok/s on one RTX 5090" and TensorFold 1.2.6/1.2.7: watch, qualify next

- **What it is:** a 38-second video claim of about 700 tok/s on one RTX 5090 with Qwen3.8-27B. The same author
  earlier reported about 220 tok/s single-stream NVFP4 decode and >500 tok/s at 4 concurrent requests. The likely
  reading is an aggregate across parallel requests. The setup is not in the post text, and we could not verify it.
  The engine is [TensorFold](https://github.com/ashhart/TensorFold) (Apache-2.0, written in Zig).
- **Local lens:** NVFP4 is Blackwell-only hardware math, so the 5090 numbers do not transfer to a 3090. Still,
  TensorFold moved this week. v1.2.5 (Nov 8), v1.2.6 (Nov 10) and v1.2.7 (Nov 11) are out, and the README now
  says the CUDA release images include sm_86. It also says sm_80 to sm_89 is not model-qualified, with no served
  checkpoint on record and FP8/NVFP4 fallback code for dense Qwen. The kernels are built with CUDA 13, and
  TensorFold refuses older drivers.
- **Verdict: watch, qualify next.** We have tracked TensorFold since October, waiting for a 3090 path, and this is
  the first release claiming one. Next steps: check that the v1.2.7 Linux archive starts on Titan's driver, then
  measure a dense Qwen3.8-27B row on the 3090 against our llama.cpp numbers.

## NInfer on RTX 5090: no value for us yet (watch)

- **What it is:** [Neroued/ninfer](https://github.com/Neroued/ninfer) (Apache-2.0), a from-scratch C++/CUDA
  engine for a few checkpoints: Qwen3.6/3.8-27B, Qwen3.6-35B-A3B, Qwen3.8-Flash-Next with CPU expert offload,
  and GLM-5.3-Flash on two DGX Sparks. It uses CUDA graphs, MTP/DFlash2 drafting and shared-prefix caching.
- **Local lens:** it builds only for `sm_120a` (RTX 5090) and `sm_121a` (DGX Spark), and its fast path is NVFP4.
  Neither of our cards can run it.
- **Verdict: watch** for an sm_86 or sm_70 target. Added to our engine watchlist.

## RustingStrata: DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash on a 12 GB RTX 3060: test as an engine

- **What it is:** [RustingStudio/RustingStrata](https://github.com/RustingStudio/RustingStrata), a pure-Rust
  GGUF inference engine (no ggml) for Qwen3 to Qwen3.8, **Qwen3.8-Flash-Next**, GLM-5.3-Flash and
  DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash. CUDA kernels are compiled at run time with NVRTC, and the same kernels also run on Vulkan.
  MoE experts that do not fit in VRAM run on the CPU at the same time as the GPU work, and placement adapts to the
  routing it observes. It has MTP drafting, a q8 KV cache, parallel slots, and OpenAI- and Anthropic-compatible
  APIs. The DeepSeek demo streams experts from SSD at 2 to 3.6 tok/s with 4K context and no tool calls, so that
  part is a stunt. The Qwen path is the interesting one. The author reports 113 tok/s decode against llama.cpp's
  66 on a 12 GB RTX 3060 with a 35B-A3B model whose experts do not fit.
- **Licence:** RustingStrata License 1.0. It allows use and modification, including commercial use. It requires
  credit to the author and forbids reselling the engine itself. That is fine for a lab.
- **Local lens:** Flash-Next with experts split between the 3090 and RAM is exactly our daily driver (Unsloth
  UD-Q2_K_XL on the Strata engine, about 121 tok/s greedy decode on the 3090 at 128K). RustingStrata lists
  Ampere and Volta as supported. It needs the CUDA 12 NVRTC for the V100, which matches Titan's driver.
- **Started:** we built a container image on Saturn from the v1.2.1 tag (commit `d5afeb03`), compiled for Titan's
  Zen 3 CPU on a CUDA 12.9 runtime, and copied it to Titan. Two A/B cases run on the same UD-Q2_K_XL file as our
  daily driver, the same card, power limit and context, against the existing Strata control: `0jf` with MTP off
  and `0jg` with one MTP draft per step. Both run greedy benchmarks plus needle retrieval at 30K and 120K.
- **Results:** pending.
